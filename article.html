FTTx Network Deployment: Bridging the Last Mile

By Annie Thomas | Published 2026

In the modern telecommunications landscape, the need for high-speed, reliable internet has never been greater. As an Electronics and Computer Engineering student with hands-on experience in fiber optic communication, I have seen firsthand how Fiber to the x (FTTx) networks are transforming connectivity. FTTx is not just a technology; it is the backbone of modern digital infrastructure, replacing traditional copper networks to deliver unparalleled bandwidth and low latency.

The Core Components of FTTx

An FTTX deployment depends on different hardware materials to satisfy its functionality. the first material is the Optical Line Terminal (OLT) which acts as the starting point of signal transmission. Then the cables are run frrom there through the distribution network which have splitters to divide a signal to multiple customer premises. At the customer premises those cables are connected to the Optical Network Unit (ONU) or Optical Network Terminal (ONT) which converts light signal back to electrical signals. the conversion is important because it is the langauge user devices like routers and computers can understand. Understanding how these components are connect is very crucial especially to any network technician. A typical FTTx deployment relies on a precise ecosystem of hardware. At the central office, the Optical Line Terminal (OLT) acts as the starting point, managing signal transmission. From there, fiber optic cables extends through the distribution network, often utilizing passive optical splitters to divide a single signal to multiple customer premises. At the customer end, the Optical Network Unit (ONU) or Optical Network Terminal (ONT) converts the optical signals back into electrical signals that routers and computers can understand. Understanding how these components interact is critical for any network technician.

The Deployment Process

During my industrial attachments at Escom OFC (Otical Fibre Communication), i learned that FTTx deploying is a procedural process. It begins with site survey and route and network planning, where engineers figures out the most efficient routes both in the systems and on site after network request. After finding the best possible route, the the optical fibre cables are run from nearest FAT to the customer premises. These cables are connected in the process called splicing which is done both at the FAT and customer premises when connecting them to the the Optical Network Unit (ONU) or Optical Network Terminal (ONT). The whole process is done carefully to prevent the micro-bends that can degradethe signal. During my internship experience, I learned that deploying an FTTx network is a meticulous process. It begins with a site survey and network planning, where engineers map out the most efficient cable routes. Next comes the physical installation of the fiber optic cables, which requires careful handling to avoid micro-bends that can degrade the signal.

The optical fibre splicing process is the most critical process as it aims at reducing signal loss.It requires materials such as cleaver, Tstrippers, cleaning wipes, sleeves, cable cutter, cable tie and splicing machine. Before the process itself i had to know the color code for optical fibre cores. Then using the fusion splicer, i aligned the fibre cores which i striped and cleaned and fused them together using an electric arc. The process requires careful attention and concentration when especially when testing individual cores before fusing them together. if there is more loss, i had to cut the core and stripe again, clean it and then fuse it again until the loss is in acceptable range.While one team is physically installing the cables, the NOC team was responsible for adding the customers in the system through network configurations. The systems are used to monitor network performance and usage and power in dofferent araes in malawi once physical installation was done, the internet access was verified at the customer site until it was successful. It was really a good experience for me and my friends there.

Challenges and Lessons Learned

Network deploying also has some challenges and these include i. Physical network damage Mostly occured due to fibre cut where the fibre cable has been cut intentionally or by accidents. For example, we had a major fibre cut in Liwonde that disrupted services for major customers. ii. Broken cores Occured due to poor splicing and it occured in Zomba where we found broken core inside the junction box as well as in Blantyre which caused network outage in both places. iii. Customer abscence This happens when customers do not notify the team that they will not be available. This delayed our installation process as we had to return again for installation. iv. Risk of fiber damage from electricity teams replacing poles Solutions i. Effective splicing and repair We re-spliced cut and broken cores inside the junction boxes and cut fibres effectively until the network was restored ii. in case of outage ordered by electricity team, we went there to repair our cables iii. Effective communication We called customers before going to their premises to know if they will be available. Lessons Learned i. I gained practical skills in fibre splicing, climbing poles, raising cables to safer positions, network configuration( VPN and FTTx) ii. i teamwork is crucial during field work as it helps to prevent accidents iii. Being humble helped me to learn a lot of work
